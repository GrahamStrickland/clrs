If $f(n) = \ceil{\lg{n}}!$ is polynomially bounded, we have $f(n) = O(n^k)$ for some 
constant $k$, i.e., there exist positive constants $c$, $n_0$, $k$ s.t. 
$0 \leq \ceil{\lg{n}}! \leq cn^k$ for any $n \geq n_0$. Now, if we let $m = \ceil{\lg{n}}$,
we have
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        m - 1 &< \lg{n} &&\leq m \\
        \Leftrightarrow 2^{m - 1} &< 2^{\lg{n}} &&\leq 2^m \\
        \Leftrightarrow 2^{m - 1} &< n &&\leq 2^m \\
        \Leftrightarrow c{(2^{m - 1})}^k &< cn^k &&\leq c{(2^m)}^k \\
        \Leftrightarrow c{(2^k)}^{m - 1} &< cn^k &&\leq c{(2^k)}^m.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

From Striling's approximation, we have
\[
    m! = \sqrt{2\pi m} {\biggl(\frac{m}{e}\biggr)}^{m}
        \Biggl(1 + \Theta\Biggl(\frac{1}{m}\Biggr)\Biggr),
\]
which, since it has a factor $m^m$, must grow more quickly than ${(2^k)}^m$ for $m > 2^k$,
so that we may conclude that $f(n) \neq O(n^k)$ for any such constant $k$, i.e., 
$\ceil{\lg{n}}!$ is not polynomially bounded.
