% Exercise 4.2-3

A matrix product does not decompose row by row: each entry $c_{ij} = \sum_k
a_{ik}b_{kj}$ depends on a whole row of $A$ and a whole column of $B$, so
stripping off the first row and column and recursing on the remainder does not
compute $C$. Instead we pad the operands out to a power of two, which the
partitioning of equations (4.9) does accept, and discard the padding afterwards.

Let $m = 2^{\ceil{\lg{n}}}$ be the smallest power of 2 with $m \geq n$.

\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \TITLE{\textsc{Strassen-Multiply-Padded}$(A, B)$}
  \STATE $n = A.\mathit{rows}$
  \STATE $m = 2^{\ceil{\lg{n}}}$
  \STATE let $A'$ and $B'$ be new $m \times m$ matrices with every entry 0
  \FOR{$i = 1$ \TO $n$}
  \FOR{$j = 1$ \TO $n$}
  \STATE $a'_{ij} = a_{ij}$
  \STATE $b'_{ij} = b_{ij}$
  \ENDFOR
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $C' = \textsc{Strassen-Multiply}(A', B')$
  \RETURN the leading $n \times n$ submatrix of $C'$
\end{algorithmic}

The padding is correct because the added rows and columns are entirely zero:
for $i, j \leq n$ we have
\[
  c'_{ij} = \sum_{k = 1}^m a'_{ik}b'_{kj}
  = \sum_{k = 1}^n a_{ik}b_{kj} + \sum_{k = n + 1}^m 0 \cdot 0
  = c_{ij},
\]
so the leading $n \times n$ submatrix of $C'$ is exactly $AB$.

For the running time, note that $m$ is the smallest power of 2 at least $n$, so
$m < 2n$. Copying the entries costs $\Theta(m^2)$, and
\textsc{Strassen-Multiply} on an $m \times m$ matrix costs
$\Theta(m^{\lg{7}})$, which dominates. Hence the total is
\[
  \Theta(m^{\lg{7}}) = O\bigl({(2n)}^{\lg{7}}\bigr)
  = O\bigl(2^{\lg{7}} \cdot n^{\lg{7}}\bigr) = O(n^{\lg{7}}),
\]
since $2^{\lg{7}} = 7$ is constant, and the product is trivially
$\Omega(n^{\lg 7})$, so the algorithm runs in $\Theta(n^{\lg{7}})$ time.
